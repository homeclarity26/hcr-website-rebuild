The Stow Homeowner Situation

Incomparable bids aren't a contractor problem. They're a documentation problem.

Stow has a wide variety of home types — established neighborhoods from the 1960s through the 1980s, newer builds on the east side, and everything in between. What they share is the same problem: homeowners who are ready to invest in their properties and can't get coherent, comparable pricing from the contractor market.

When contractors write their own scopes, bids are incomparable by design. Contractor A includes the electrical panel upgrade; Contractor B doesn't. Contractor C uses a different finish specification entirely. The only way to make bids genuinely comparable is to give every contractor the same documented scope — one that was written by an independent expert with no stake in the outcome.

That's what the Home Clarity Report produces. You stop choosing between apples and oranges. You start making an informed decision.

Common Questions

Stow homeowners ask us:

I've already gotten three bids. Two are close, one is way off. Which do I trust?

This is exactly what the Report clarifies. Adam produces an independent scope and price range for your project. You compare all three bids against that benchmark — and you'll immediately see whether the outlier is inflated, missing something, or actually the most complete bid. Without a documented reference point, you're guessing.

My home is from the 1970s. Is it too old for the Report to be useful?

The opposite. Homes from this era are approaching the end of original system lifespans — HVAC, plumbing, electrical, roofing, windows. The Report's system assessment is most valuable precisely in homes like this, where multiple systems may be approaching replacement simultaneously and the sequencing question becomes critical for budget planning.

How is this different from a home inspection?

A home inspection tells you what's broken today. The Home Clarity Report tells you what to do about it, in what order, and what it costs — from someone who's completed 400+ renovation projects in Summit County. See the full comparison →
