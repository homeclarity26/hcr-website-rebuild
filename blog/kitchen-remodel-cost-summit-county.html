Kitchen Remodel Cost in Summit County, Ohio: What to Actually Budget in 2026

April 2026 · By Adam Kilgore

The number one question I get from homeowners considering a kitchen renovation is: "How much is this going to cost?" My honest answer is always the same: it depends. But it doesn't depend on random variables — it depends on specific, knowable factors. I can tell you exactly what those are after 27 years pricing these projects in Summit County.

Here's what a kitchen remodel actually costs in Northeast Ohio in 2026, broken down by scope. These are real numbers from real projects — not national cost-estimator averages that have nothing to do with your home or your market.

Summit County Kitchen Remodel Cost Ranges

ScopeTypical RangeWhat's Included
Cosmetic refresh$12,000 – $25,000Cabinet reface, new countertops, hardware, fixtures, paint
Mid-range renovation$35,000 – $65,000New semi-custom cabinets, countertops, appliances, flooring, lighting
Full gut renovation$65,000 – $120,000+Layout changes, new plumbing/electrical, custom cabinets, everything new

These ranges apply to typical Summit County homes — Hudson, Bath Township, Fairlawn, Cuyahoga Falls, Stow, and surrounding communities. They account for current Northeast Ohio labor rates and 2026 material pricing. They are not what you'll find on national estimator sites, which consistently run 15–25% below actual local costs.

What Drives the Price Up

Layout changes

Moving your sink, dishwasher, or refrigerator requires relocating plumbing, electrical, and sometimes structural elements. A load-bearing wall removal adds $4,000–$10,000 before you touch a single cabinet. If you want an open-concept kitchen, budget for that specifically and get a structural engineer involved early — not after demo.

Cabinet quality

This is the single biggest cost variable in a kitchen remodel. Stock cabinets from a big-box store run $80–$250 per linear foot installed. Semi-custom from a local cabinet shop is $250–$550. Full custom is $550–$1,100+. The difference is real and visible — but stock cabinets specified correctly and installed well can look excellent. Don't overspend on cabinet quality if your budget is tight. Spend on layout and function instead.

Countertops

Laminate runs $15–$40 per square foot. Quartz is $65–$120. Granite is $55–$95. Quartzite or marble is $80–$150+. For a typical Summit County kitchen (roughly 35–50 sq ft of countertop), expect $1,800–$12,000 depending on material. Quartz is the sweet spot for most clients — durable, consistent, lower maintenance than natural stone.

Appliances

A realistic mid-range appliance package (refrigerator, range, dishwasher, microwave hood) runs $4,500–$9,000. Entry-level packages start around $3,000. Luxury brands — Sub-Zero, Wolf, Miele, Thermador — add $15,000–$40,000 to the package. Appliances are where the most scope creep happens. Set your budget here early and hold to it.

Electrical and plumbing

If you're keeping the layout and your existing systems are in acceptable condition, these costs are modest — $1,200–$3,500 for updates, outlet additions, and code compliance. If you're adding an island with a prep sink, or upgrading from a 30A to a 50A circuit for a new range, budget $3,500–$8,000 more. If your panel needs an upgrade to support the new load, add another $2,500–$4,500.

What Drives the Price Down

Keeping the existing layout is the single biggest cost saver — often $8,000–$20,000 compared to a full repositioning. The second lever is choosing quality where it shows (countertops, cabinet faces) and being pragmatic where it doesn't (cabinet box material, drawer slide brand). Third: making decisions before the project starts. Change orders mid-project add 15–30% to the final cost, consistently.

Real example: A Hudson client came to me with a $45,000 kitchen budget. The contractor they'd spoken to had quoted $62,000. The difference wasn't contractor markup — it was that the contractor had assumed a layout change and upgraded appliances the client didn't need. A clear, written scope of work saved them $17,000 before a single cabinet was ordered.

What You're Not Getting Priced For (But Should Be)

The bids you're collecting almost certainly don't include everything. Common omissions from Summit County kitchen bids:

  • Permit fees ($400–$1,200 depending on township)
  • Structural discoveries during demo — in homes built before 1990, this is common
  • Temporary kitchen setup during construction
  • Paint in adjacent spaces affected by the renovation
  • Flooring that extends into an open dining or living area
  • Electrical panel upgrade if capacity is marginal

These aren't hidden costs contractors add maliciously — they're genuinely outside the scope as typically written. A thorough scope of work defines what's in and what's not, so there are no surprises.

Why Your Three Bids Are So Different

You call three contractors. You get $38,000, $61,000, and $52,000. All three contractors walked the same kitchen. The bids are different because they're pricing different things — different cabinet quality assumptions, different layout interpretations, different contingency approaches, different subcontractor relationships. Without a written scope of work, these bids are incomparable.
